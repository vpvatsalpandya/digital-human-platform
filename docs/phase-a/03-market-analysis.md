# A-03 · Market Analysis

Figures below are planning estimates built bottom-up from public enrolment data and list
prices; each carries the assumption it rests on. They are inputs to the revenue model (A-08),
not audited market research. Refresh annually.

## 1. Who buys, who uses

| Segment | Buyer | User | Purchase trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical colleges (MBBS, BDS) | Dean / Head of Anatomy / IT procurement | Students, faculty | Curriculum reform (CBME), NMC/NAAC inspection, cadaver shortage, new college opening |
| Nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy, allied health colleges | Principal | Students | Accreditation, competitor adoption, student outcomes |
| Universities with health faculties (UK, EU, ANZ, ME, SEA, Africa) | Faculty lead + procurement | Students, faculty | Replacing an expiring incumbent licence; VLE/LMS integration mandate |
| Schools (grades 9–12, biology) | School group / board | Students, teachers | Digital classroom initiatives |
| Exam-prep (NEET, PG entrance, USMLE, PLAB) | Student (B2C) or coaching institute | Students | Exam calendar |
| Hospitals / teaching hospitals | Medical education department | Residents, nurses | CME requirements, onboarding |

## 2. Sizing (bottom-up)

### India (beachhead)
- ~780 medical colleges, ~118,000 MBBS seats per year → ~550,000 MBBS students across
  4.5-year cohorts (assumption: steady growth to ~130,000 seats by 2028).
- ~320 dental colleges, ~28,000 BDS seats per year.
- Nursing: ~5,000+ institutions, ~300,000+ annual seats across GNM/BSc.
- Pharmacy, physiotherapy, allied health: ~600,000+ enrolled.
- NEET-UG applicants: ~2.3 million per year; a small fraction converts to paid prep.

Serviceable market at institutional pricing (A-08): 1,100 medical/dental colleges × average
contract USD 18k = USD 20M; nursing/allied at USD 4k average × 6,000 = USD 24M; schools and
B2C are upside. **Indian SAM ≈ USD 45–50M ARR at maturity.**

### Global
- ~3,900 medical schools worldwide (WDMS); ~1.5 million medical students; nursing and allied
  health several times that.
- Incumbent list prices (public): Complete Anatomy ~USD 75/yr individual, institutional
  site licences typically USD 10–60k; Visible Body individual ~USD 30–60/yr, institutional
  by FTE; BioDigital enterprise custom. Universities already spend; the switch cost is the
  content workflow, not the price.
- **Global SAM ≈ USD 900M–1.2B ARR** (institutional anatomy/physiology digital tools plus
  assessment). Our 36-month target of USD 9M ARR is < 1% penetration.

## 3. Demand drivers
1. Competency-based curricula shifting hours from didactic to self-directed learning that
   needs interactive material.
2. Cadaver scarcity and cost; body-donation ethics rules; COVID-era normalisation of
   virtual dissection.
3. Assessment burden: spotters and OSPEs are labour-intensive; digital stations cut examiner
   hours and produce analytics regulators increasingly ask for.
4. Device reality: in India >90% of students own an Android phone; laptop ownership is far
   lower. Incumbents' mobile apps are 2–6 GB downloads that exclude budget devices.
5. Institutional procurement prefers multi-year SaaS with SSO/LMS integration over per-student
   app purchases.

## 4. Headwinds
- Long procurement cycles (6–12 months) in public universities.
- Faculty conservatism; "cadaver is irreplaceable" (true — we position as complement).
- Price sensitivity in Tier-2/3 Indian colleges; addressed by department-level licences.
- Content trust: a single anatomical error discovered by a professor kills a deal. Review
  workflow and citations are a sales feature, not a compliance chore.

## 5. Regional entry sequence
1. **India** (months 0–12): reference customers, NEET/MBBS mode, Hindi + regional UI.
2. **South-East Asia, Middle East, Africa** (12–24): English-medium colleges, similar device
   constraints, price-sensitive; regional resellers.
3. **UK / EU / ANZ** (18–36): LTI, SAML, GDPR residency, procurement frameworks.
4. **US** (30+): FERPA, accessibility (Section 508), heavy incumbent presence; enter via
   nursing/allied and PA programmes rather than head-on with medical schools.
